Every subscription funds the next discovery.

The Research Pledge is our public commitment to the researchers who use Nelview, and to the research ecosystem we're part of.

Our five commitments

We hold ourselves to these, and we'll report on them publicly every year.

01

We fund African research.

A share of every Nelview subscription goes into a fund for prototype-phase and data-collection research across Africa.

02

We evaluate. We never write.

Nelview will never generate or rewrite your research. Feedback points to problems; fixing them is your work.

03

Your work is never training data.

Documents are deleted after review, never used to train models and never sold or shared.

04

Reviewers are paid and credited.

Every expert reviewer is paid for their time, and can be credited for their contribution when they choose to be.

05

Scores are explainable.

Every score comes with the criteria behind it. We publish how bands are set so no one is judged by a black box.

Good ideas stall at the first cheque.

Across African universities, promising projects stop at the same point: money for a first prototype or a season of fieldwork. The pledge exists to close that gap, funded by the researchers and institutions who use Nelview.

How the fund works.

Simple enough to explain in four steps, and audited every year.

1You subscribe

A share of every Pro and Institution subscription is set aside automatically.

2The fund pools

Contributions are held separately from company operating funds.

3The board awards

Our academic board reviews applications and awards grants on merit.

4Results stay open

Funded work stays owned by its researchers, and outcomes are reported publicly.

What it funds.

The two stages where early research most often runs out of money.

Prototype-phase research

Early work that needs a working proof of concept before larger funders will engage.

Data collection

Fieldwork, surveys and lab time: the costly stage where promising projects most often stall.

Mentorship alongside

Every grant comes with a matched mentor from our reviewer network.

Who can apply.

Open to researchers based at African institutions, at any stage.

01

Students

Final-year undergraduates and master's students with a supervised project.

02

PhD and early-career researchers

Doctoral candidates and postdocs within five years of their PhD.

03

University teams

Small groups led by a faculty member, including cross-institution teams.

When do grant applications open?
The first call opens once the fund has pooled enough for a full round. Sign up below and we'll email you the moment it does.
Do I need to be a Nelview user to apply?
No. Grants are awarded on merit to any eligible researcher at an African institution.
Who owns the research a grant funds?
You do. Nelview takes no ownership, rights or co-authorship in funded work.
Who decides which projects are funded?
Our academic board reviews every application against published criteria. Board members step aside from any application where they have a conflict of interest.

Be first to hear when grants open.
