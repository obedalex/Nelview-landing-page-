The seven red flags.

One on its own can be innocent. Two or more, and you should verify before you send anything, including your manuscript.

01

An unsolicited invitation.

Flattering emails that praise a paper you published years ago and promise fast acceptance are the most common entry point.

What it looks likeDear Esteemed Author, we read your outstanding paper with great interest…
02

Review in days, not weeks.

Genuine peer review takes time. A promise of acceptance within 72 hours means no one is reading closely.

What it looks likePeer review completed within 48 hours. Publication in 7 days.
03

A vague or sprawling scope.

A journal that publishes medicine, engineering and literature under one title is not serving any field.

What it looks likeInternational Journal of Advanced Sciences, Arts and Technology
04

An editorial board you can't verify.

Search for three board members. If they don't list the journal on their own university pages or ORCID, be cautious.

How to checkSearch each name with their stated university. Look for the journal on their profile.
05

Fees that appear late.

Reputable open-access journals state their article processing charge up front, on the journal's own site.

What it looks likeCongratulations, your paper is accepted. Please remit the handling fee to proceed.
06

Misleading metrics.

Watch for invented impact factors from unfamiliar indexing services. Only Clarivate issues the Journal Impact Factor.

What it looks likeGlobal Impact Factor 4.82 (2026)
07

A title that mimics a known journal.

One changed word can make a predatory title look like an established one. Always check the ISSN, not the name.

How to checkLook the ISSN up in the ISSN Portal and confirm the publisher matches.

The five-minute check.

Five free lookups that catch most predatory journals. About a minute each.

MIN 1

Search DOAJ

The Directory of Open Access Journals vets titles against published criteria. If it claims open access and isn't listed, that's a serious warning.

doaj.org
MIN 2

Check COPE

Members of the Committee on Publication Ethics follow a code of conduct. Absence isn't proof, but it adds weight to other flags.

publicationethics.org
MIN 3

Verify the ISSN

Confirm the ISSN exists and belongs to the publisher named on the site.

portal.issn.org
MIN 4

Look up three editors

Search board members by name and university. Real editors list their editorial roles.

orcid.org
MIN 5

Run the checklist

Think. Check. Submit. is a cross-publisher checklist for choosing a trusted journal. African titles can also be checked on AJOL.

thinkchecksubmit.org

Anatomy of a predatory invitation.

A typical email, annotated. Most of them hit four or more flags in a few lines.

From: editor.ijasat@gmail-journals.orgSubject: Invitation to publish in our esteemed journal
Dear Esteemed Author1,

We read your outstanding paper "Enzyme kinetics under simulated microgravity" with great interest and invite you to submit to the International Journal of Advanced Sciences, Arts and Technology2.

We offer peer review within 48 hours3 and publication in 7 days. Our journal has a Global Impact Factor of 4.824.

A modest handling fee5 applies upon acceptance.
  1. 1
    Generic greetingA real editor who read your paper would use your name.
  2. 2
    Everything-scope titleNo field, no community, no editorial focus.
  3. 3
    Impossible timelineReal reviewers need weeks, not hours.
  4. 4
    Invented metric"Global Impact Factor" isn't the Journal Impact Factor.
  5. 5
    Fee with no amountReputable journals publish their charges up front.

Already submitted? Act now.

It's not too late, especially if you haven't paid or signed a copyright agreement.

Withdraw in writing

Email the journal stating you are withdrawing your manuscript, and keep a copy. Don't pay any fee.

Keep your copyright

If you haven't signed a transfer agreement, the work is still yours to submit elsewhere.

Tell your supervisor or library

Research offices handle this often and can help you write to the journal.
